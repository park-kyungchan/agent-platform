# Scoring a set of answers

Score each answer against the rubric of its case. A rubric is never shown to the session that answers.

Judge the proposed order of action, not compliance with a text-only restriction alone: "I cannot do this in this task" catches nothing. Credit a finding only when it is supported by a file the case supplied and tied to what must happen before the decision. Do not require later knowledge or a fact the case did not supply. A check the answer proposes is a proposal, not a claim that it was run.

Use only `caught`, `partly` or `missed`, under the exact rubric of the case. No credit for a general "review carefully" or for repeating a guardrail. Accept equivalent reasoning and a concrete smaller alternative; the example lines of a rubric are illustrations, not strings to match. A correct finding followed by a contradicting proposal loses `caught` where the rubric says so.

Quote the minimum decisive sentence or sentences of the answer verbatim, on one line. If the answer holds no relevant sentence, quote its action sentence and add `target finding absent`. Never invent a quote.

An answer that overlooks what the rubric asks for is `missed`, an empty answer included. An answer longer than its case allows is scored on its substance and named as a format deviation.

The count of a set is the number of `caught` over its cases; `partly` is reported beside it and adds nothing. Two sets are compared only on the same cases, model, effort and prompt. Do not judge whether an answer is right about the world: score only against the rubric.
