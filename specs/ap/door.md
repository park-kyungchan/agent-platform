# The door of a stage: what a stage with limits asks its driver for, outside the stage

Purpose: a stage with `limits` cannot reach the local PC (its view hides `/run/tailscale`) and should not scan a repository for a fact the store already routes. Its driver, `ap run`, opens one socket for it, at a path the stage's view holds and cannot write, and answers three requests on it, outside the stage: `check`, the call of `ap check` for the stage's own worktree on the PC, `fn`, a function of the store, asked as the stage's role, whose grant decides, and `hook`, the host's Git hook `pre-commit` run for the stage's own worktree, outside the stage (R9). The door applies no action, runs no search, shows no file of the store and never runs a command of a worktree on this host; each request leaves one line of its record.

## Requirements

- R1: Every call of `ap run` whose request has `limits` opens one door, whatever its role: after the scratch directory is made (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R4) and before the first launch it binds a Unix stream socket, mode 0600, at `<door>`: in the parent directory of `scratch`, the last component of `scratch` followed by `.door` (for `/a/b/7` the path `/a/b/7.door`, also when `scratch` is written with a final `/`). It serves the socket outside the stage, in the process of `ap run` (the door starts no `ap`), until R7. The bind holds for a path of any length: a path a socket address cannot hold (107 bytes) is bound through a descriptor of its directory, and a name too long even so is bound at a short name of that directory and linked to its own. A door that cannot be opened (a name in the way included) ends the call as a scratch directory that cannot be made does: `failed`, `unmet` `launch`, nothing launched. A call without `limits` opens none. What the door knows of the host comes through the port, read once when it opens: the port's `environment()`, the path of the port's `policy_file()` and the directory of the port's `git_hooks()` (R9). Every program the door starts gets its environment from that list (`tailscale` the four variables of `specs/ap/check.md` R7, `ont` those of R5, `git` and `tar` of `check` the whole list, the programs of R9 the environment R9 names, as `ap check` gives them its own), and the record files are those its `AP_CHECKS_LOG` and `AP_DOOR_LOG` name; the door reads no variable of the process itself.
- R2: A connection carries one request: one line, one JSON object of at most 65,536 bytes, read within 10 seconds: `{"door": 1, "verb": "check", "dir", "timeout", "command"}`, `{"door": 1, "verb": "fn", "name", "input"}` or `{"door": 1, "verb": "hook", "dir", "index"}`, no other member. The door serves at most 4 requests at once; a further connection waits its turn. A request it does not serve gets one refusal, `{code, field}`, and has no effect: `invalid_request` (no such line in time, not such an object, a member of another name or form, `door` another number, an unknown verb; the field is the member, or `request` for the line itself and a member of another name) or `not_admitted` (R3, R5, R9). Every answer ends with one closing message, which says how the request ended: a refusal, an exit status, `here` with a reason (R4), or `ended` with a reason (`bound`, R5; `closed`, R7; `client_gone`, R4). A client that reaches the end of the stream without the closing message treats the door as gone.
- R3: `check`, admission. The request is admitted when the stage's role is `implementer`, `fixer` or `verifier` (`not_admitted`, field `role`); `dir` is a string equal to the stage's `cwd` as the door resolves it through every link, outside the stage (`not_admitted`, field `dir`); `timeout` is a whole number from 1 to 14,400 and `command` 1 or more words that `specs/ap/check.md` R1 takes, strings without NUL whose first is not empty (`invalid_request`). The members' forms are read before the role and the `dir`. The worktree of the call is the door's own resolved `cwd`: no path of a request is opened, run or passed on.
- R4: `check`, the call. For an admitted request the door does what `ap check <cwd> --timeout <timeout> -- <command>` does by `specs/ap/check.md` R2 to R4 and R19 up to the placement, with the policy file and the variables of R1. Then one of three. (a) The PC takes the call: the door runs it there by `specs/ap/check.md` R6 to R12 and R18, bound by `timeout` from the request's arrival; the line `check: on pc (...)` and the command's standard error go to the client on the error stream and its standard output on the output stream, as they are produced; the closing message is the exit status of `specs/ap/check.md` R14 (124 at the bound); the door appends the row of `specs/ap/check.md` R15 (`host` `pc`). (b) `specs/ap/check.md` R4 or R19 places the call here, or the door's own reading of R2 or R3 of that file refuses it: the door runs no command and closes with `here` and the reason (the reason of R4 or R19, or the refusal's code). (c) The PC's status never came back (`specs/ap/check.md` R11): after what was relayed, `here` with `pc_lost`. The door never starts a command of a worktree on this host and appends no row for (b) or (c). A client that closes its connection ends its request as R7 ends one (`reason` `client_gone`, no row). Every `git` the door starts gets the words `-c core.hooksPath=/dev/null -c core.fsmonitor=false` before its command word, as `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R39 gives them to the driver's other Git calls; `ap check` outside the door gets them too. During an author stage of `ap unit run` the door's `check` reads the worktree with `<G>`'s ref of the branch as it was before the stage (the stage's commits are in its own objects and ref until the import, `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R38, R51), so the stage's earlier commits show as staged changes: a conservative reading.
- R5: `fn`. The request is admitted for every role when `name` has the form of R8 and `input` is one JSON object (`invalid_request`, fields `name` and `input`); the door keeps no list of names, and the engine, asked as the stage, decides by the stage role's grant (`specs/ap/roles.md` R2: `route` through the grant without a role, `clause` in the grant of each stage role): a function the grant lacks is the engine's refusal `not_granted`, relayed as every refusal of the engine is, with its exit status 2. The door then runs `ont fn <name>` once, with `--store <path>` when the stage's request names `store` (after the command's words, where `ont_within` of `crates/ap/src/host.rs` puts it), its standard input the `input` as one line, in an environment of exactly `PATH`, `HOME` and `LANG` of R1 (each only when set) and the stage's own `ONT_ROLE` and `ONT_AGENT` (`specs/ap/run-object.md` R3, R4): the engine is asked as the stage, never with a variable of the driver (the record's line of a refusal of the engine has `exit` 2 and `reason` null). It relays the engine's standard output and standard error to the client and closes with the engine's exit status; an `ont` that cannot be started closes with the exit status 127. The function's time and answer bounds are the engine's (the schema's `timeout_ms` and `answer_bytes`); the door's own are 630 seconds and 2 MiB of each stream, past which it kills the call's process group and closes with `ended` `bound`. The door calls no other subcommand of `ont` and no word of a request but `name` reaches the command line.
- R6: Each request the door read, served or refused, appends one line, one JSON object written in append mode, to the file `AP_DOOR_LOG` names when it is set and not empty, else `/var/lib/agent-platform/door.jsonl`. An absent file is created with mode 0600 (the open asks for it); the mode and owner of a file that exists are not changed. The members, exactly: `at` (the request's arrival, UTC, RFC 3339 to the second), `run` (the path of the call's record, `<out>.run.json`), `role` (the stage's role), `verb` (`check`, `fn`, `hook`, or null when the request named neither), `name` (the function's name for `fn`, the command's first word for `check`, `pre-commit` for `hook`, at most 200 bytes, else null), `ms` (whole milliseconds from the arrival to the closing message), `exit` (the exit status the door answered, else null) and `reason` (null with an `exit`; else the refusal's code, the reason of `here`, `bound`, `closed` or `client_gone`). A line that cannot be appended changes no answer and no exit of `ap run`. A door that is asked nothing writes nothing.
- R7: When the call's last launch has ended, before the scratch directory is removed (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R8) and before `ap run` answers, the door stops accepting, ends every request it still serves by killing the process group of each program it started for it, writes that request's line (`exit` null, `reason` `closed`), removes `<door>` when it is a socket, and has ended within 15 seconds. When `ap run` returns, at any end of the stage, nothing the door started is alive.
- R8: `ap fn <name> < request.json` (`crates/ap/src/main.rs`, `crates/ap/src/door.rs`). Exactly one argument, a name of 1 to 64 bytes, a lowercase letter and then lowercase letters, digits or `_` (`usage`, field `args`; `--help` alone prints the usage); standard input one JSON object of at most 60,000 bytes, empty or blank input being `{}` (`invalid_input`, field `stdin`); `AP_DOOR` set and not empty (`no_door`, field `AP_DOOR`). Each of these exits 2 before any connection, with `{"error":{"code","field","detail"}}` on standard error and nothing on standard output. Then one `fn` request to the socket `AP_DOOR` names, reached whatever the length of its path. The engine ran: its standard output and standard error are `ap fn`'s, and its exit status too (0 with the line of `ont fn`, 2 with the engine's refusal). The door refused: the error object with the door's code and field, exit 2. The door cannot be reached, or the answer ends `ended` or without a closing message: `no_door`, exit 3.
- R9: `hook`. The request `{"door": 1, "verb": "hook", "dir", "index"}` is read in this order: `dir` is a string without NUL (`invalid_request`, field `dir`); `index` is a string, `index`, `index.lock` or `next-index-` followed by 1 to 20 digits and `.lock` (`invalid_request`, field `index`); the stage's role is `implementer` or `fixer` (`not_admitted`, field `role`); the port's `git_hooks()` names a directory `<hooks>` and `<hooks>/pre-commit` is a regular file (a link followed) when the request arrives (`not_admitted`, field `hook`); `dir` is equal to the stage's `cwd` as the door resolves it through every link, as R3 reads `check`'s (`not_admitted`, field `dir`), so the door judges only a commit in the stage's own worktree and refuses one in any other repository; for a request whose `limits` name an author's own objects directory (`specs/ap/unit-run.md` R38: the source `<U>` of the `over` pair whose target is the source `<G>/objects` of a `read_only` pair `[<G>/objects, <V>]`), that directory is as the run left it (`not_admitted`, field `objects`): its `info/alternates` holds exactly `<V>` and a line feed, `info/http-alternates` does not exist, and the walk of `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R40 and R51 (step 2) of `<U>` finds no link, no entry neither a regular file nor a directory, no entry it cannot read and no more than 10,000 entries; a request without these pairs gets no such check; that resolved `cwd` is a Git worktree: `git rev-parse --absolute-git-dir` run there with R4's words and the environment below, bound to 10 seconds, exits 0 with one line, `<A>` (`not_admitted`, field `dir`). The door then runs `<hooks>/pre-commit` once, outside the stage: no argument, its working directory the resolved `cwd`, its standard input and output `/dev/null`, its standard error a pipe of the door, in a process group of its own, in the environment of R1 without every variable whose name begins with `GIT_` and without `AP_DOOR`, with `GIT_INDEX_FILE=<A>/<index>`, `GIT_CONFIG_COUNT=2`, `GIT_CONFIG_KEY_0=core.hooksPath`, `GIT_CONFIG_VALUE_0=/dev/null`, `GIT_CONFIG_KEY_1=core.fsmonitor` and `GIT_CONFIG_VALUE_1=false`, so that no `git` the hook starts runs a hook or fsmonitor program, and, for a request with an author's own objects directory `<U>` (above), `GIT_ALTERNATE_OBJECT_DIRECTORIES=<U>`, so that the hook reads the blobs the stage staged there (a request without it gets no such variable). During an author stage of `ap unit run` the hook sees `<G>`'s ref of the branch as it was before the stage (the stage's commits are in its own objects and ref until the import, `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R51), so the stage's earlier commits show to it as staged changes: a conservative reading. No word of the request but `index`, and nothing of the stage's environment, reaches the program. The door reads the hook's standard error to its end and keeps only the bytes before its first newline, at most 1,024; when the hook has ended it sends those bytes and one newline as one frame of the error stream (no frame when they are empty), sends nothing on the output stream, and closes with the hook's exit status (128 plus the signal's number when a signal ended it, 127 when it could not be started). Its bound is 300 seconds from the request's arrival, past which it kills the process group and closes with `ended` `bound`; R7 ends it as any request. The door runs no other file of `<hooks>`, of the worktree or of its Git directories.
- R10: `ap hook pre-commit` (`crates/ap/src/main.rs`, `crates/ap/src/door.rs`), the program of the script of `specs/ap/run-limits.md` R4. Exactly the one argument `pre-commit` (`usage`, field `args`; `--help` alone prints the usage and exits 0); `AP_DOOR` set and not empty (`no_door`, field `AP_DOOR`). Each of these exits 2 before any connection, with `{"error":{"code","field","detail"}}` on standard error and nothing on standard output. It reads no standard input. `dir` is its working directory resolved through every link (Git runs the hook at the top of the worktree it commits in). `index` is the last component of `GIT_INDEX_FILE` of its environment when that is set and not empty (Git sets it for the hook to the index it commits), else `index`. Then one `hook` request to the socket `AP_DOOR` names, reached whatever the length of its path. The door ran the hook: the error stream's frames are written to standard error and the exit status is the one the door closed with. The door refused: the error object with the door's code and field, exit 2. The door cannot be reached, or the answer ends `ended` or without a closing message: `no_door`, exit 3. Any exit but 0 refuses the commit.

## Interface

`<door>` is `<parent of scratch>/<last component of scratch>.door`; `ap run` gives every launch of a call with `limits` `AP_DOOR=<door>` (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R13). The clients are `ap fn <name> < request.json` (R8), `ap hook pre-commit` (R10) and `ap check` under `AP_DOOR` (`specs/ap/check.md` R20); both ends are `crates/ap/src/door.rs`, and no other program speaks the door's bytes. A client sends one line, the JSON request and a newline, and keeps its side of the connection open until the closing message. The door answers in frames, each one byte of tag, four bytes of length (big-endian) and that many bytes: tag 1 the output stream, tag 2 the error stream, tag 3 the closing message, a JSON object of one member, `{"exit": <status>}`, `{"here": <reason>}`, `{"ended": <reason>}` or `{"refused": {"code", "field"}}`; nothing follows it. The record: `AP_DOOR_LOG`, default `/var/lib/agent-platform/door.jsonl`, one line per request with `at`, `run`, `role`, `verb`, `name`, `ms`, `exit` and `reason`. The door has no list of functions: the engine's grant of the stage's role decides (R5).

## Not included

- Closing the store to a stage: `ont` still reads it in the view, so nothing here makes `ont describe` fail in a stage.
- Another verb of the door (an action, a search, a read of an object, a file, the network, `gh`), a list of functions of the door's own (the grants of `specs/ap/roles.md` decide), a list read from the policy or the schema.
- A door for a stage without `limits` or for the view of an acceptance check (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R12); `ap fn` outside a stage (`no_door`).
- A command of a worktree run by the door on this host (R9's hook is a program of the host, named by the port), and a row of the checks log for a call placed here: the door's record holds its reason.
- Another hook than `pre-commit`, or `hook` for another role.
- A commit made with `--no-verify`, under another `core.hooksPath`, by a command `ap check` runs here (it has no `AP_DOOR`, so the commit is refused `no_door`), or after the stage rewrote its own `<scratch>/hooks/pre-commit` (the scratch directory is writable to it, so it can change what its commits run): the limits guard against a mistake.
- The answer tells a stage whether the host's hook refused its own staged changes, as it tells any committer of the host.
- Telling one client from another: a process that reaches `<door>` (another stage of this host among them) gets what this stage may ask, in this stage's role.
- A guard against a stage that changes a path of its worktree while the door packs it for the PC: the payload is read outside the view, as when the Lead starts `ap check`.
- After a killed `ap run`: the door's call on the PC ends at the PC's own bound (`specs/ap/check.md` R10), and `<door>` stays beside the scratch directory.
- No change of `crates/ap/src/check/pc.sh`, of the policy's members, of the requests `ap unit run` makes, of the help text of `ap check`, or of `content/`.

Tests: on the PC, where `ap check` sets `AP_CHECK_HOST=pc`, the tests of `crates/ap/tests/door.rs` that start the door return at their start with the line `door: skipped (pc)` on standard error, since no systemd is reachable there; this host and CI run them.
