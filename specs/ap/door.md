# The door of a stage: what a stage with limits asks its driver for, outside the stage

Purpose: a stage with `limits` cannot reach the local PC (its view hides `/run/tailscale`) and should not scan a repository for a fact the store already routes. Its driver, `ap run`, opens one socket for it, at a path the stage's view holds and cannot write, and answers two requests on it, outside the stage: `check`, the call of `ap check` for the stage's own worktree on the PC, and `fn`, a function of the store on the door's list, asked as the stage's role. The door applies no action, runs no search, shows no file of the store and never runs a command of a worktree on this host; each request leaves one line of its record.

## Requirements

- R1: Every call of `ap run` whose request has `limits` opens one door, whatever its role: after the scratch directory is made (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R4) and before the first launch it binds a Unix stream socket, mode 0600, at `<door>`: in the parent directory of `scratch`, the last component of `scratch` followed by `.door` (for `/a/b/7` the path `/a/b/7.door`, also when `scratch` is written with a final `/`). It serves the socket outside the stage, in the process of `ap run` (the door starts no `ap`), until R7. The bind holds for a path of any length: a path a socket address cannot hold (107 bytes) is bound through a descriptor of its directory, and a name too long even so is bound at a short name of that directory and linked to its own. A door that cannot be opened (a name in the way included) ends the call as a scratch directory that cannot be made does: `failed`, `unmet` `launch`, nothing launched. A call without `limits` opens none. What the door knows of the host comes through the port, read once when it opens: the port's `environment()` and the path of the port's `policy_file()`. Every program the door starts gets its environment from that list (`tailscale` the four variables of `specs/ap/check.md` R7, `ont` those of R5, `git` and `tar` the whole list, as `ap check` gives them its own), and the record files are those its `AP_CHECKS_LOG` and `AP_DOOR_LOG` name; the door reads no variable of the process itself.
- R2: A connection carries one request: one line, one JSON object of at most 65,536 bytes, read within 10 seconds: `{"door": 1, "verb": "check", "dir", "timeout", "command"}` or `{"door": 1, "verb": "fn", "name", "input"}`, no other member. The door serves at most 4 requests at once; a further connection waits its turn. A request it does not serve gets one refusal, `{code, field}`, and has no effect: `invalid_request` (no such line in time, not such an object, a member of another name or form, `door` another number, an unknown verb; the field is the member, or `request` for the line itself and a member of another name) or `not_admitted` (R3, R5). Every answer ends with one closing message, which says how the request ended: a refusal, an exit status, `here` with a reason (R4), or `ended` with a reason (`bound`, R5; `closed`, R7; `client_gone`, R4). A client that reaches the end of the stream without the closing message treats the door as gone.
- R3: `check`, admission. The request is admitted when the stage's role is `implementer`, `fixer` or `verifier` (`not_admitted`, field `role`); `dir` is a string equal to the stage's `cwd` as the door resolves it through every link, outside the stage (`not_admitted`, field `dir`); `timeout` is a whole number from 1 to 14,400 and `command` 1 or more words that `specs/ap/check.md` R1 takes, strings without NUL whose first is not empty (`invalid_request`). The members' forms are read before the role and the `dir`. The worktree of the call is the door's own resolved `cwd`: no path of a request is opened, run or passed on.
- R4: `check`, the call. For an admitted request the door does what `ap check <cwd> --timeout <timeout> -- <command>` does by `specs/ap/check.md` R2 to R4 and R19 up to the placement, with the policy file and the variables of R1. Then one of three. (a) The PC takes the call: the door runs it there by `specs/ap/check.md` R6 to R12 and R18, bound by `timeout` from the request's arrival; the line `check: on pc (...)` and the command's standard error go to the client on the error stream and its standard output on the output stream, as they are produced; the closing message is the exit status of `specs/ap/check.md` R14 (124 at the bound); the door appends the row of `specs/ap/check.md` R15 (`host` `pc`). (b) `specs/ap/check.md` R4 or R19 places the call here, or the door's own reading of R2 or R3 of that file refuses it: the door runs no command and closes with `here` and the reason (the reason of R4 or R19, or the refusal's code). (c) The PC's status never came back (`specs/ap/check.md` R11): after what was relayed, `here` with `pc_lost`. The door never starts a command of a worktree on this host and appends no row for (b) or (c). A client that closes its connection ends its request as R7 ends one (`reason` `client_gone`, no row). Every `git` the door starts gets the words `-c core.hooksPath=/dev/null -c core.fsmonitor=false` before its command word, as `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R39 gives them to the driver's other Git calls; `ap check` outside the door gets them too.
- R5: `fn`. The request is admitted for every role when `name` is in the door's list, one constant of `crates/ap/src/door.rs` that holds `route` and nothing else (`not_admitted`, field `name`), and `input` is one JSON object (`invalid_request`). The door then runs `ont fn <name>` once, with `--store <path>` when the stage's request names `store` (after the command's words, where `ont_within` of `crates/ap/src/host.rs` puts it), its standard input the `input` as one line, in an environment of exactly `PATH`, `HOME` and `LANG` of R1 (each only when set) and the stage's own `ONT_ROLE` and `ONT_AGENT` (`specs/ap/run-object.md` R3, R4): the engine is asked as the stage, never with a variable of the driver. It relays the engine's standard output and standard error to the client and closes with the engine's exit status; an `ont` that cannot be started closes with the exit status 127. The function's time and answer bounds are the engine's (the schema's `timeout_ms` and `answer_bytes`); the door's own are 630 seconds and 2 MiB of each stream, past which it kills the call's process group and closes with `ended` `bound`. The door calls no other subcommand of `ont` and no word of a request but `name` reaches the command line.
- R6: Each request the door read, served or refused, appends one line, one JSON object written in append mode, to the file `AP_DOOR_LOG` names when it is set and not empty, else `/var/lib/agent-platform/door.jsonl`. An absent file is created with mode 0600 (the open asks for it); the mode and owner of a file that exists are not changed. The members, exactly: `at` (the request's arrival, UTC, RFC 3339 to the second), `run` (the path of the call's record, `<out>.run.json`), `role` (the stage's role), `verb` (`check`, `fn`, or null when the request named neither), `name` (the function's name for `fn`, the command's first word for `check`, at most 200 bytes, else null), `ms` (whole milliseconds from the arrival to the closing message), `exit` (the exit status the door answered, else null) and `reason` (null with an `exit`; else the refusal's code, the reason of `here`, `bound`, `closed` or `client_gone`). A line that cannot be appended changes no answer and no exit of `ap run`. A door that is asked nothing writes nothing.
- R7: When the call's last launch has ended, before the scratch directory is removed (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R8) and before `ap run` answers, the door stops accepting, ends every request it still serves by killing the process group of each program it started for it, writes that request's line (`exit` null, `reason` `closed`), removes `<door>` when it is a socket, and has ended within 15 seconds. When `ap run` returns, at any end of the stage, nothing the door started is alive.
- R8: `ap fn <name> < request.json` (`crates/ap/src/main.rs`, `crates/ap/src/door.rs`). Exactly one argument, a name of 1 to 64 bytes, a lowercase letter and then lowercase letters, digits or `_` (`usage`, field `args`; `--help` alone prints the usage); standard input one JSON object of at most 60,000 bytes, empty or blank input being `{}` (`invalid_input`, field `stdin`); `AP_DOOR` set and not empty (`no_door`, field `AP_DOOR`). Each of these exits 2 before any connection, with `{"error":{"code","field","detail"}}` on standard error and nothing on standard output. Then one `fn` request to the socket `AP_DOOR` names, reached whatever the length of its path. The engine ran: its standard output and standard error are `ap fn`'s, and its exit status too (0 with the line of `ont fn`, 2 with the engine's refusal). The door refused: the error object with the door's code and field, exit 2. The door cannot be reached, or the answer ends `ended` or without a closing message: `no_door`, exit 3.

## Interface

`<door>` is `<parent of scratch>/<last component of scratch>.door`; `ap run` gives every launch of a call with `limits` `AP_DOOR=<door>` (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R13). The clients are `ap fn <name> < request.json` (R8) and `ap check` under `AP_DOOR` (`specs/ap/check.md` R20); both ends are `crates/ap/src/door.rs`, and no other program speaks the door's bytes. A client sends one line, the JSON request and a newline, and keeps its side of the connection open until the closing message. The door answers in frames, each one byte of tag, four bytes of length (big-endian) and that many bytes: tag 1 the output stream, tag 2 the error stream, tag 3 the closing message, a JSON object of one member, `{"exit": <status>}`, `{"here": <reason>}`, `{"ended": <reason>}` or `{"refused": {"code", "field"}}`; nothing follows it. The record: `AP_DOOR_LOG`, default `/var/lib/agent-platform/door.jsonl`, one line per request with `at`, `run`, `role`, `verb`, `name`, `ms`, `exit` and `reason`. The door's list of functions: `route` (`specs/ap/route.md`).

## Not included

- Closing the store to a stage: `ont` still reads it in the view, so nothing here makes `ont describe` fail in a stage.
- Another verb of the door (an action, a search, a read of an object, a file, the network, `gh`), a function beside `route`, a list read from the policy or the schema, a grant per function.
- A door for a stage without `limits` or for the view of an acceptance check (`specs/ap/run-limits.md` R12); `ap fn` outside a stage (`no_door`).
- A command run by the door on this host, and a row of the checks log for a call placed here: the door's record holds its reason.
- Telling one client from another: a process that reaches `<door>` (another stage of this host among them) gets what this stage may ask, in this stage's role.
- A guard against a stage that changes a path of its worktree while the door packs it for the PC: the payload is read outside the view, as when the Lead starts `ap check`.
- After a killed `ap run`: the door's call on the PC ends at the PC's own bound (`specs/ap/check.md` R10), and `<door>` stays beside the scratch directory.
- No change of `crates/ap/src/check/pc.sh`, of the policy's members, of the requests `ap unit run` makes, of the help text of `ap check`, or of `content/`.
