# Roles: the grants of the work schema, the role each verb and service claims, and the roles file

Purpose: the work schema's grants give each role the actions and functions it applies and calls, instead of one grant of every action to every caller. A service that drives units can no longer approve one, a stage applies nothing, and a Lead's every write logs its role. Every apply of `ap` names its role. Among callers of root the role is a claim that the engine checks against the roles file beside the store: a guard against error, not a boundary; the boundary against a stage is the operating system and the door.

## Requirements

- R1: The grants (`ontology/work.schema.json`, schema version 23; `ontology/README.md`, "Version 22: roles", "Version 23: capacity for dispatch"). `grants` is, in this order: (a) one grant of principal `agent` without `role`, `scope` `{}`, `read` and `derive` those of the one grant of version 21, `actions` `[]` and `functions` `["route"]`, which stays `grants[0]`; (b) one grant per role of R2 in R2's order: principal `agent`, `scope` `{}`, `role`, `derive` `[]`, its `actions` and, where it has any, its `functions`, each list sorted; `read` is that of the grant without a role when the grant lists any action, else `[]` (the engine refuses a grant that applies an action without reading the types its parameters dereference, `reference_read_forbidden`). No other member of the schema differs from version 21's but `version`. An apply or a function call of a caller with no role is answered by the grant without a role: no action, and `route`.
- R2: The roles, in this order, and what each holds. `lead`: every action type and every function of the schema. `dispatch`: what the drivers apply: `ap work` (`start_unit`, `note_unit_wait`, `merge_unit`), `ap unit run` (`start_unit`, `resume_unit`, `record_head`, `stop_unit`, `accept_unit`, `reopen_unit`, `merge_unit`, `start_unit_review`, `close_unit_review`, `carry_review`), `ap unit write` (`amend_unit` and its look's `start_unit_review` and `close_unit_review`) and the runs of stages (`start_run`, `close_run`), and the function `capacity` (`ap unit run` reads it before a verifier round to place the look, `specs/ap/unit-run.md` R45). `deliver`: `start_install`, `close_install`. `watch`: `open_finding`, `see_finding_again`, `reopen_finding`, `close_finding`. `route`: nothing. `measure`: `declare_host`, `observe_host`, `observe_account` (`ap resource`), `record_session`, `extend_session`, `measure_run`, `record_measurement` (`ap measure`), and the functions `api_spend`, `capacity` and `cost` (the status page). `api`: `start_run`, `close_run` (`ap api batch`) and the finding actions of the batch reader (`open_finding`, `see_finding_again`, `reopen_finding`, `close_finding`). `implementer`, `fixer`, `verifier`, `reviewer`, `writer`: no action; the function `clause` (and `route`, through the grant without a role). A service role holds exactly what its programs apply and call, so `approve_unit`, `record_principle` and every other gate of the Lead are in `lead`'s grant alone. A unit that adds an action or a function adds it to `lead`'s grant and to each role that applies or calls it.
- R3: The claims. Every `action apply` and `action validate` request of `ap` carries the member `role`. Every `start_run` and `close_run` of a stage's run (`specs/ap/run-object.md` R7, R9, R11, R14; the close of a stopped or killed stage by `ap unit run` and by `specs/ap/unit-write.md` R4; the close of a look's reviewer) claims `dispatch` whatever verb drives the stage, the run's `role` parameter staying the stage's; those of `ap api batch` claim `api`, their `role` parameter staying `batch`. Every other apply claims the role of its verb: `lead` for `ap request` (every form, the hook's included), `ap intent`, `ap unit add|amend|ready|drop|review|approve|reject`, `ap review`, `ap row` and `ap backlog`; `dispatch` for `ap work`, `ap unit run` and `ap unit write`; `deliver` for `ap deliver`, whoever calls; `watch` for `ap watch`; `measure` for `ap resource`, `ap quota` and `ap measure`; `api` for `ap api`; `route` for `ap route` and `ap clause`. Nothing else of a request changes, and the refusals `role_in_environment` stay. `ap work` adds `--setenv ONT_ROLE=dispatch` to every `systemd-run` it makes (`specs/ap/work.md` R14, R24), so that a run's function calls claim `dispatch`. A program outside `ap` that applies to the host's store sends a fixed member `role` in each request and keeps dropping `ONT_AGENT` and `ONT_ROLE` from the engine's environment, so that an action id is the same whoever runs it: the readers of `evals/batch` (`evals/batch/lib.py`'s `Engine` takes the role from its caller) send `api`; `evals/cases/reproduce` and the suites' `score --record` send `lead`; `ontology/migrate` sends `deliver`. A call of a function other than `route` sets `ONT_ROLE` to the caller's role for that call only.
- R4: The services. Every `deploy/*.service` whose program runs `ap` or applies to the store has exactly one line `Environment=ONT_ROLE=<role>`: `ap-work` `dispatch`, `ap-deliver` `deliver`, `ap-watch` `watch`, `ap-route` and `ap-clause` `route`, `ap-resource`, `ap-quota`, `ap-status-page` and `ap-measure` `measure`, `ap-api-nightly` and `ap-api-weekly` `api`. It is the role of the service's function calls and of a program it runs that is not `ap`. `deploy/ont-backup.service` has none. `deploy/ont-serve.service` also carries no `Environment=ONT_ROLE=` line because `ont serve` authenticates remote peers over Tailscale and never inherits server `ONT_ROLE` (engine `specs/ont/serve.md` R9).
- R5: The roles file. `deploy/roles.json` maps `uid:0` to `[every role of R2, sorted]` and `ts:park-kyungchan@github` (the user's PC tailnet principal) to `["lead"]` (every role listed for a `ts:` principal must be a role of R2's grants), the form the engine reads beside the store (`<ONT_INSTALL_STATE_DIR>/ont/roles.json`, engine `specs/ont/action.md` R113). `ap deliver` with `engine` compares it with the host's file, keeps a differing regular file beside it and writes the merged bytes, or ends `roles_install_failed` (`specs/ap/deliver.md` R58, R59). The roles of `uid:0` are exactly those of the grants.
- R6: What a role is. Among callers of root a role is a claim that the engine checks against the roles file (engine action.md R112, R113): a role the file does not list is `role_not_admitted`, an apply with no role is `not_granted`. It is a guard against error and no boundary: a caller of root may claim any role the file lists. A function call claims `ONT_ROLE` alone (the MCP tool `ont_fn` the MCP server's `ONT_ROLE`), and `route` needs no role, so a Lead runs `ONT_ROLE=lead ap status --page` or `ONT_ROLE=lead ont fn <name>` for any other function; a session that works for the Lead (a worker, a subagent, a session of another CLI) claims `lead` as the Lead does. A program outside `ap` sends its fixed role (R3) and drops `ONT_ROLE`, set only for a call of a function other than `route`. The boundary against a stage is the operating system (its view holds no store, `specs/ap/run-limits.md`) and the door, which sets the stage's role itself and asks the engine as the stage (`specs/ap/door.md` R5): the stage's grant decides which function it may call, and it applies no action.

## Interface

The grants are `ontology/work.schema.json`'s member `grants`, the roles file `deploy/roles.json`, the claims the member `role` of the body of `ont action apply` and `ont action validate` and `ONT_ROLE` of `ont fn`; the tests are `crates/ap/tests/roles.rs`, `crates/ap/tests/deliver.rs` (R58, R59) and `crates/ap/tests/door.rs`, and `ontology/rehearsal.sh` asserts the grants against a real engine. The first delivery of the roles is by hand (`deploy/README.md`, "The first delivery of roles").

## Not included

- A check that each request `record_principle` names exists: a criterion tests no element of an array parameter (engine `specs/ont/action.md` R68); it needs the engine or a verb.
- Authentication of a role among root callers; a role per agent or session; tagged-node principals or per-session roles beyond the user's `ts:` principal.
- A role for the read commands, `ont index` and `action list`: the engine gives them none.
- Removing the `role_in_environment` refusals; `ONT_ROLE` in a session's or an MCP server's settings.
- Any change of the engine or its MCP tools, and of a stage's MCP configuration.
- An action for a stage role; a function list of the door's own.
- A member `role` in the requests of `evals/route/run`, which calls only `route`.
- A rollback of the roles file by `ap deliver`: by hand from its kept copy.
