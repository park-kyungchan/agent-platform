# The runtime CPU quota of the agents' slice: `ap quota`

Purpose: the host's limit on busy CPU (a share of all CPUs) holds with the slice that runs the agents, because one turn of `ap quota` sets the slice's runtime `CPUQuota` to what the limit leaves after the load outside the slice, and a timer of the service manager runs a turn about every 30 seconds.

## Requirements

- R1: `ap quota` takes no argument (an argument is `usage`, field `args`, exit 2, before any reading) and reads nothing from standard input. It is one turn: read, compute, set when the value differs, answer. It holds no lock and reads and writes no store, so two turns at once compute the same formula and the last one's value stands.
- R2: The policy file (the path `ap run` reads, `specs/ap/policy.md` R1) may hold the member `quota`, an object. `slice` is required: 1 to 32 characters of `a-z0-9` followed by `.slice` (no dash: a slice name with dashes is nested under parent slices). `busy_limit` is an integer from 10 to 100 (default 85, per cent of all CPUs), `margin` from 0 to 50 (default 5), `ceiling` from 10 to 6,400 (default 170, per cent of one CPU: the value the slice's persistent setting holds) and `floor` from 10 to `ceiling` (default 60, or the `ceiling` when that is lower). Another member inside `quota`, a member that is not an integer or is out of its range, or a `quota` that is not an object is `policy_invalid` (field `policy`, the detail begins with `quota`, exit 2); a file that cannot be read or is not one JSON object is `policy_invalid` too, the detail beginning with `file`. Nothing is read or set after one.
- R3: No policy file, or a file without `quota`, answers `{"skipped":"no quota"}` (exit 0) and makes no other call. When the host has no service manager (`System::units` is false) the answer is `{"skipped":"no service manager"}` (exit 0) and no other call is made; a `quota` that is invalid is refused first (R2).
- R4: The load is read twice from `/proc/stat`, ten seconds apart. Its line `cpu ` gives the first eight numbers; `idle` is the fourth alone, so time waiting for I/O counts as busy, as `mpstat` counts it. The CPUs are the lines `cpu<n>`. `busy` = 100 × (1 − Δidle / Δtotal), as an integer per cent of all CPUs (rounded to the nearest).
- R5: The slice's use is read at the same two moments: `systemctl show -p ControlGroup --value <slice>`, then `usage_usec` of `/sys/fs/cgroup<ControlGroup>/cpu.stat`. `slice_use` = Δusage_usec / Δmicroseconds between the two moments (the host's monotonic clock) × 100, as an integer per cent of one CPU (rounded to the nearest). It is 0 unless both answers of `ControlGroup` are exactly `/<slice>` (a slice with no unit yet, or stopped, answers an empty string, and `/sys/fs/cgroup/cpu.stat` is the whole host's), both files were read and the second count is not lower than the first. A file that is absent counts as no use.
- R6: `outside` = max(0, `busy` × CPUs − `slice_use`), per cent of one CPU. A use read as 0 makes `outside` larger and the quota lower, never higher.
- R7: The value now is `systemctl show -p CPUQuotaPerSecUSec --value <slice>`: `infinity` is null; else one or more terms separated by spaces, each `<n>min`, `<n>s`, `<n>.<digits>s` (at most six digits) or `<n>ms`, and the per cent is the seconds × 100 (`600ms` is 60, `1s` 100, `1.050000s` 105, `1min 4s` 6,400). A value that is not a whole number of per cent differs from every computed value (the answer shows it cut to a whole number). Any other text is `reading_failed`.
- R8: `/proc/stat` unreadable or without its line, with fewer than eight numbers, with no `cpu<n>` line or whose total does not grow between the readings (`reading_failed`, field `proc_stat`), a call of `systemctl show` that fails or prints no readable value (`control_group`, `quota_now`) and a `cpu.stat` that is unreadable for another reason than being absent, or without `usage_usec` (`cpu_stat`) are `reading_failed`, exit 3, and nothing is set.
- R9: `quota` = (`busy_limit` − `margin`) × CPUs − `outside`, held between `floor` and `ceiling`, an integer.
- R10: When `quota` differs from the value now (or that is null) the turn calls `systemctl set-property --runtime <slice> CPUQuota=<quota>%`, then reads the value again; a value that is not `quota` is `set_failed` (exit 3, `detail` the value read, null for infinity; a call that fails is `set_failed` with field `set_property`). When it does not differ the turn calls nothing that changes.
- R11: The calls of a turn are exactly `systemctl show -p ControlGroup --value <slice>` (twice), `systemctl show -p CPUQuotaPerSecUSec --value <slice>` (once, twice after a set) and the `set-property` of R10, each on the one slice the policy names. It never removes the runtime setting, never sets another property, never acts on another unit, starts, stops or reloads nothing. The time it reads is the host's, through the same port as every other call.
- R12: The answer is one JSON line on standard output, exit 0: `at` (UTC to the second), `slice`, `cpus`, `busy`, `slice_use`, `outside`, `before` (the value now, or null), `quota` and `set` (true when it set). A refusal or failure is `{"error":{"code","field","detail"}}` on standard error: exit 2 before any reading (`usage`, `policy_invalid`), exit 3 after.
- R13: `deploy/ap-quota.service` is `Type=oneshot` with `ExecStart=/usr/local/bin/ap quota`, `Environment=HOME=/root`, `TimeoutStartSec=90` (not less than the 80 seconds a turn can take at most: the wait of 10 between the readings, four calls bound to 10 and one to 30) and no `Slice=`: the service runs outside the slice it sets, so the quota does not throttle it. `deploy/ap-quota.timer` has `OnBootSec=1min`, `OnUnitInactiveSec=20s`, `AccuracySec=1s` and `WantedBy=timers.target`: a turn takes ten seconds of reading and twenty of rest.
- R14: `ap run` does not read `quota`: a policy file with the member admits and refuses a stage exactly as the same file without it (`specs/ap/policy.md`).

## Interface

`ap quota`, no argument; the member `quota` of the policy file as R2 says; the answers of R12. One reading function of `crates/ap/src/quota.rs` (`load`) gives `cpus`, `busy`, `slice_use` and `outside` of a slice; the turn adds the value now and the set. The install, the state and the undo are in `deploy/README.md`, "The CPU quota".

## Not included

- A rise of the load outside the slice passes the limit for at most one turn: the quota is computed from the load of the ten seconds before it is set.
- When the load outside leaves the slice less than `floor`, the slice keeps the floor and the limit is not held until that load falls.
- A reload of the service manager returns the slice to its persistent value (the runtime setting is dropped) until the next turn.
- A policy that names another slice stops the turns on the first one: a renamed slice keeps the last runtime value a turn set on it, and nothing returns it. The undo in `deploy/README.md` therefore names every slice a policy has named since the install.
- While the timer is stopped, and while turns fail, the last value stays; the unit `ap-quota.service` then reads `failed`.
- No loop, no lock, no record in the store (the journal holds the answers), no dispatch of units, no memory or account limit, no install: a Lead installs the two files.
